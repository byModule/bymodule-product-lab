# Metrics

*Version française : [README.fr.md](README.fr.md)*

From October 2026, I publish usage figures for the Module learning platform every month and revenue every quarter. This page explains what is counted, where the numbers come from, and the rules I follow. No figures are published yet: the first file is due on 2 November 2026.

## What will be published

**Usage, every month**, in one file per month named `YYYY-MM.md`:

| Figure | What it counts |
| --- | --- |
| Visits from social networks and GitHub | Visits to bymodule.com that come from LinkedIn, other social networks and this repository |
| Sign-ups | Accounts created on the platform |
| Completion of the free course | Share of learners who started the free first course and completed it |
| Purchases | Number of purchases (Pass, learning paths, individual courses), as a count |
| Qualified feedback | Feedback that is specific enough to act on, from Discussions, the platform, comments and interviews |

**Revenue, every quarter**: published in January for October to December, in April for January to March, and so on.

## Where the numbers come from

- **Usage**: Google Analytics 4, read through the audience page of the platform's administration (read only), with campaign parameters on the links I share to tell the sources apart.
- **Purchases and revenue**: Stripe, which handles payments.
- **Feedback**: Discussions in this repository, the platform, comments on my posts, and learner interviews once they start.

Each monthly file states the period, the source of each figure, and any change in how a figure is measured.

## Known limits

- Analytics hits from bymodule.com were blocked by the site's content security policy until a fix released on 6 October 2026 (see the [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md)). October usage figures therefore cover part of the month only, and the October file will say so.
- Analytics only counts visitors who accept measurement. The figures are a lower bound, not a census.
- Small numbers move a lot from one month to the next. I will show raw counts next to any percentage.

## The honesty rule

Bad numbers are published too. A month with few visits, a low completion rate or no purchases gets the same file as a good month, with what I think explains it and what I will change. Figures are not rounded up, and a measurement mistake is corrected in the file where it appeared, with a note.
